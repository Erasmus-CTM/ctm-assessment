# ctm-assessment: Turn course notes into a place to practise

Bring writing, mathematics, Python and interactive diagrams together on a Quarto page. Students can try an idea, check their work and ask for help while staying close to your explanation.

[Try the activities](https://erasmus-ctm.github.io/ctm-assessment/example.html) · [Example to adapt](example.qmd) · [Authoring guide](docs/authoring.md)

## Try it, then make it yours

Explore the examples as a student: change an answer, run a calculation, or improve a short piece of writing. Then open the source beneath an activity and adapt it for your course.

Choose one small learning goal. Start with an example that comes close, replace its task and draft, and decide how much help students should receive.

## Ideas for your teaching

- Pair a diagram with a mathematics question or a language activity.
- Let students complete a short Python function and check it themselves.
- Compare a gentle hint with a worked explanation of the same problem.
- Give feedback using the terminology and methods taught in your course.

## Choose the kind of help

A useful hint leaves the student something to do. You can ask for one question,
a short explanation, or a review focused on a particular skill. The same task
can offer different help at different stages of learning.

**Feedback** can prepare a message to paste into an AI chat you already use.
If your course has an AI service set up, it can show the reply on the exercise
page. AI advice is for discussion and revision; it does not replace your
judgment or the exercise's checks.
